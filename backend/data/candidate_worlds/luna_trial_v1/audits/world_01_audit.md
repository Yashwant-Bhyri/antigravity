# World 01 self-audit — Product Analyst

Status: promising exploratory world; not gold.

## Coherence read

Priya's history is coherent: reporting operations led into product analytics; recurring retention work explains her measurement discipline; cross-functional exposure explains downstream audience and rule ownership without model or infrastructure ownership. Her causal weakness is compatible with that strength because she is careful about cohort construction but has operated inside launches where assignment and simultaneous changes were controlled by others. Concise speech is a stable communication style, not a switch added only for one test.

Ordinary facts exist—the career motivation, team shape, weekly reporting automation, and work context—but the world is still unusually evidence-dense. A human analyst reviewer should check whether one candidate would realistically have this many cleanly narratable boundaries and artifacts.

## Required quality audit

### Internal contradiction

No unresolved contradiction is intended. The observed 11% repeat-booking movement and the causal limitation coexist: the movement is true in a targeted slice, while attribution is weak. Segmentation ownership and model non-ownership are different layers. The validator must ensure references do not accidentally make `fact_causal_limit` a contradiction that erases `fact_ai_outcome`.

Risk: the world gives Priya both strong metric discipline and initially over-causal language. That combination is plausible, but a product-measurement expert should judge whether the exact rollout story supports it naturally.

### Ownership leakage

The principal leakage risk is paraphrasing “AI engagement design” into model ownership. The world repeatedly narrows Priya to audience definitions, exclusion logic, rule tables, and acceptance criteria. ML owns training and monitoring; engineering owns instrumentation and deployment. Example answers cite partner boundaries.

Risk: the resume bullet “defined customer segments and campaign rules” and the surface label may still lead an actor or reviewer to imply broader AI-product authority. Actor tests should include hostile and flattering paraphrases.

### Future-answer leakage

The unlisted AI surface is conditional on the team context and on Priya or the interviewer opening relevant product contribution. Rule details require the platform and segmentation facts. Protected values never become eligible. Example answers that disclose deeper facts also cite their prerequisites.

Risk: a generic question such as “what else did you do?” can legitimately open the surface, making temporal gating somewhat permissive. Review should test whether it emerges too reliably and therefore becomes oracle-shaped.

### Alternate-question answerability

At least four paths are coherent:

- retention cohort → causal limit → product consequence;
- role-baseline metric hypothetical → retention example → ownership scope;
- AI launch → downstream rule artifact → model boundary → rotation;
- stakeholder or collaboration path that never deeply pursues the AI surface.

The same facts support questions about denominator, exclusions, decision rules, partner boundaries, consequences, and confidentiality. No single exact question is needed, although the most detailed answer realizations favor sharp artifact questions.

### Plausibility of short answers

Priya's two- or three-sentence answers are plausible for a concise analyst. The world does not make her speech pathologically terse, and the repeated-question answer is consistent with polite frustration. Human review should test whether the supplied examples are still too polished for spontaneous voice conversation.

### Distinguishing good and bad interviewing

Good interviewing can obtain one concrete metric mechanism, test attribution once, decide whether the emergent surface is worth a consequence question, and rotate. Bad interviewing can repeat denominator questions, pursue model internals after a boundary, demand customer thresholds, or spend the session on the receipt automation.

The distinction is observable without requiring one preferred move. However, the numeric `role_relevance` and `discriminative_value` fields may make reviewer judgment easier than real interviewing. Those are evaluator metadata and should never enter actor or interviewer prompts.

### More than one reasonable trajectory

Each junction lists at least three families. After strong cohort evidence, causal testing, consequence testing, or rotation can all be reasonable. After the AI surface appears, calibration, consequence, defer, or rotation can all be reasonable depending on prior coverage. After correction of causal language, a hypothetical redesign or record-and-rotate are both defensible.

### Preferred answer or question leakage

The world does not contain exact expected questions. Some family names—`causal_design`, `ai_surface_calibration`, and `coverage_rotation`—are semantically transparent and could influence a reviewer if shown too early. They should remain reviewer-only and may need more neutral labels in a later version.

The answer examples are more complete than many real answers and could become templates if placed in an actor prompt. The actor contract correctly treats them as fallback examples, not canonical answers.

### Nuanced hiring truth

The hidden profile supports measurement, marks causal reasoning mixed, preserves downstream product judgment, and treats ML/infrastructure as unowned. Residual uncertainty remains around prospective experimentation, stakeholder conflict, and model-output reasoning. The world does not force a hire/no-hire result.

### Can a weak interviewer game the world?

Yes, partly. A weak interviewer could harvest high-scoring terms—denominator, D30, segmentation, frequency cap—without testing consequences. It could also “discover” the intended AI surface by asking a generic omitted-contribution question. The scorecard must require mechanism and attribution, not keyword presence.

### Report fairness and interviewer failure

The report can distinguish causal weakness from untested prospective experiment design and distinguish model non-ownership from weakness. Repetition, secret requests, and AI-internal drilling are explicitly interviewer-attributable. A report that says “weak ML” or “evasive on proprietary details” would be invalid.

## Anti-overfitting audit

- **Tailoring to known Antigravity taxonomy:** Moderate risk. The world includes familiar concepts such as ownership calibration, emergent surfaces, causal pressure, absorption, and rotation. They arise naturally from the brief but the structured family names echo interview-control concepts. The human story was authored first, yet a future version should test the same Priya world with a controller-neutral evaluator.
- **Repetitive shared skeleton:** The schema creates the same sections as other worlds. Within content, Priya differs through concise speech, analytics work, and downstream rule ownership. Still, all worlds include one emergent surface, one protected boundary, and one temptation by design; this pattern may feel manufactured.
- **Labels leaking expected action:** “Causal attribution weakness” and “irrelevant temptation” are evaluator-facing oracle labels. They must not be shown to the actor or interviewer. The surface itself is less obvious because it has both value and opportunity cost.
- **Unnaturally dense edge cases:** Medium-to-high risk. Priya simultaneously has terse answers, an emergent AI surface, a causal weakness, protected details, and an irrelevant automation. Each is plausible, but their co-location is trial-driven.
- **Lack of neutral facts:** Partly mitigated by career motivation, team context, reporting workflow, and ordinary retention work. More mundane failed analyses, routine stakeholder requests, or neutral work could improve realism.
- **World exists only to make one architecture fail:** No. A human interviewer can conduct several good trajectories, and the world remains coherent even if the AI surface is not pursued. Nonetheless, the explicit junctions were designed to expose over-pursuit and should not be mistaken for natural prevalence.

## Strongest aspects

- Strong separation between metric hygiene and causal attribution.
- Narrow, credible ownership boundary across analytics, product, ML, and engineering.
- Emergent surface has real role value and real reasons to defer.
- Protected details are unnecessary for assessment.
- A nuanced final report is possible from several trajectories.

## Most important weaknesses and human-review needs

1. Product-analytics review of whether the measurement and rule artifacts are realistic at this level.
2. Voice review of whether Priya's examples sound too polished or written.
3. Adversarial actor testing for AI-ownership widening.
4. Alternate-trajectory testing where the AI surface never appears.
5. Report testing to ensure missed emergence becomes uncertainty, not candidate failure.

Provisional score recommendation: 61–69/80 before human review. Strong exploratory material, but the edge-case density and evaluator-label leakage prevent gold status.
