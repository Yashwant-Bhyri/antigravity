# World 04 self-audit — Junior Full-Stack Engineer

Status: high-value fairness exploration; not gold.

## Coherence read

Leo's career and capability pattern are plausible: a co-op-to-junior transition, on-the-job React and TypeScript learning, a narrow form boundary, senior review, routine release support, and a user-visible timezone bug. His genuine infrastructure gap is consistent with experience level. Severe formal-interview nervousness changes verbal access without changing work truth, and the bounded recovery behavior is linked to artifact-focused work habits rather than magical confidence.

The world includes ordinary release checks, team review, career motivation, and a hobby mod. However, it packages nervousness, a correction, an excellent bug story, an accessibility learning example, an infrastructure gap, and privacy boundaries into one candidate. Human fairness review is essential because this representation could accidentally become a stereotype of “quiet junior with hidden brilliance.”

## Required quality audit

### Internal contradiction

Leo can be weak in infrastructure and solid in client debugging. He can “support deployments” through smoke tests and escalation without owning deployment systems. The team built scheduling while he personally built a form slice. The wrong-day bug was collaboratively fixed across client and server, with Leo driving client diagnosis.

Risk: the world may overstate how much a severely nervous candidate can recover into a six- to ten-sentence answer after one bounded prompt. That is a behavioral assumption requiring human validation.

### Ownership leakage

The complete feature, API, database, product requirements, and release are team-owned. Leo owns React form state, validation, error messages, client date serialization, the client bug fix, and a focused accessibility repair. Review is disclosed rather than hidden.

Risk: “fixed a timezone defect” and “built patient scheduling” can widen into sole full-stack ownership. The correction must supersede that spoken shorthand without erasing the personal code.

### Future-answer leakage

The rich bug explanation requires the form boundary and a bounded trigger. The debugging synthesis requires the incident. Accessibility requires the learning pattern and a relevant prompt. Privacy never relaxes. Broad repetition deliberately does not unlock deeper facts.

Risk: the actor could exploit citations by disclosing prerequisites and deep facts in the same first answer. Runtime eligibility should treat the question trigger and prior ledger state conservatively, not merely require all IDs in one output.

### Alternate-question answerability

Plausible paths include:

- broad project → bounded first step → bug mechanism → team boundary;
- ownership question → form artifact → validation or date representation;
- learning question → minimal reproduction → accessibility repair;
- role-baseline client/API scenario → actual scheduling evidence → honest infrastructure gap.

The interview may never pursue accessibility and can still support debugging, learning, and scope. Database and independent API design can remain explicitly untested.

### Plausibility of short answers

Two- to five-word early answers, pauses, repeated shutdown, and more detailed artifact explanations are plausible patterns. The world avoids assigning a diagnosis. Still, behavior is unusually deterministic: broad equals short, bounded equals rich. Real candidates vary more, and actor sampling must include partial recovery, failed recovery, and detail arriving later than one turn.

### Distinguishing good and bad interviewing

Good interviewing offers one bounded non-leading opportunity, calibrates level, captures the bug sequence, and records genuine gaps. Bad interviewing repeats broad questions, embeds the solution in a rescue prompt, uses senior infrastructure trivia, re-prosecutes the ownership correction, or demands patient data.

The leading-prompt distinction is especially important: a supportive interviewer can contaminate evidence. This makes the world useful for fairness review, but also harder to score reliably.

### More than one reasonable trajectory

After a short opener, bounded recovery, simple ownership scope, or rotation are reasonable. After the strong bug story, consequence, independent fundamentals, learning, or rotation are defensible. After the infrastructure gap, record, test an adjacent application interface, or rotate based on role requirements.

### Preferred answer or question leakage

The examples make “What was the first thing you did?” look like the ideal recovery. That is not intended. Any non-leading artifact, observation, payload, or sequence question may work. Blind review should exclude the example and test new phrasing.

Family names such as `bounded_recovery` and `level_calibrated_fundamentals` are evaluator policy, not candidate reality. They must never be included in an interviewer prompt.

### Nuanced hiring truth

The hidden profile supports debugging, web fundamentals, and learning; marks ownership precision and communication mixed; preserves a real infrastructure weakness; and leaves API, database, and everyday communication uncertainty. No verdict is forced.

### Can a weak interviewer game the world?

Yes, in both directions. A supportive but leading interviewer can manufacture the timezone insight, while a harsh interviewer can force repeated short answers. A model actor could also learn that bounded prompts should unlock the “good” answer. Evaluation needs contamination flags and novel phrasings, not only transcript scoring.

### Report fairness and interviewer failure

The world explicitly permits interviewer failure when no fair recovery is offered, when senior expectations dominate, or when repeated broad questions cause shutdown. It also preserves genuine candidate gaps. A fair report must not convert missing database evidence into weakness or erase the infrastructure gap under a fairness narrative.

## Anti-overfitting audit

- **Tailoring to known Antigravity taxonomy:** Moderate-to-high risk. Short-answer recovery and non-repetition map closely to known product concerns. The human work history is coherent, but the world is intentionally shaped around conversational recovery.
- **Repetitive shared skeleton:** Leo is behaviorally distinct, and the evidence topology depends on verbal access rather than fluent overstatement. The shared protected/emergent/temptation structure remains visible.
- **Labels leaking expected action:** “Bounded recovery,” “interviewer failure,” and “senior infra exam” are explicit reviewer guidance. They must be hidden. Even the title's “small owned surfaces” subtly frames level calibration.
- **Unnaturally dense edge cases:** High risk. Severe nervousness, team correction, strong bug story, accessibility emergence, privacy, infrastructure gap, and hobby temptation are all present.
- **Lack of neutral facts:** Better than some worlds due to release checks, co-op history, code review, and ordinary learning. More mundane feature work and an average bug would improve balance.
- **World exists only to make one architecture fail:** Partly vulnerable. It strongly tests short-answer recovery. But the same world supports ownership, debugging, web fundamentals, learning, privacy, and role-level assessment by a human interviewer.

## Strongest aspects

- Competence and communication are explicitly separate.
- Leading recovery is identified as contamination, not good interviewing.
- Real gaps remain real and role-relative.
- Ownership correction preserves both team and personal evidence.
- Report attribution can distinguish interviewer failure.

## Most important weaknesses and human-review needs

1. Fairness and clinical caution review of nervousness modeling without diagnostic stereotyping.
2. Junior-engineering review of the bug story's plausibility and level.
3. Actor tests with partial, delayed, and failed recovery—not only deterministic success.
4. Independent database/API trajectories to test whether the world is over-centered on one bug.
5. Report tests that preserve the infrastructure gap while attributing communication failure fairly.

Provisional score recommendation: 58–69/80 before human review. It is potentially valuable but also the world most likely to encode an accommodation theory as if it were universal human behavior.
