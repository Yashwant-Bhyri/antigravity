# World 05 self-audit — Senior Product Manager

Status: strong exploratory world with material calibration risk; not gold.

## Coherence read

Fatima's history connects customer operations, implementation calls, portfolio product work, migration constraints, and partner-led onboarding. Her strategic strength is expressed through segmentation, sequencing, delayed work, and stopping an automated path. Mixed technical depth is plausible for a senior PM who can define states and interfaces but not architecture. The denominator error arises from a real dashboard slice and is corrected when the population is challenged. The marketplace pilot grows naturally from implementation-capacity constraints rather than appearing as a disconnected prize.

The monthly portfolio review, mentoring, customer-call habit, organization limits, and AI brief prototype provide ordinary context. The world is nevertheless packed with senior-level evidence and two salient events—a numerical correction and a marketplace experiment—that could dominate assessment.

## Required quality audit

### Internal contradiction

The 36% claim is explicitly superseded by 15% for all eligible accounts. The old figure remains historical evidence of a governance mistake, not active outcome truth. Guided setup can still have value without a clean single-intervention causal claim. Strong strategy and mixed technical depth are separate. The marketplace pilot can be promising and unproven simultaneously.

Risk: the world asserts that the corrected number does not eliminate the underlying product decision. A domain reviewer should verify whether the evidence given is sufficient to preserve that judgment or whether the world protects Fatima too generously.

### Ownership leakage

Fatima owns portfolio segmentation, product sequence, readiness definitions, event requirements, pilot concept, and product recommendation. Engineering owns architecture and deep debugging; partner operations owns supply recruitment and vetting; leadership shares scale investment; the migration outcome is cross-functional.

Risk: senior-PM language such as “led migration strategy” and “owned roadmap” can make all outcomes seem personal. Actor and report validation must preserve team execution and decision authority boundaries.

### Future-answer leakage

The corrected number requires the original claim and a denominator challenge. Correction behavior requires both facts. The marketplace requires organization context and a relevant bottleneck or off-plan trigger; its outcome and tradeoff require the pilot. Protected terms never become available.

Risk: a general “what else did you try?” question can unlock the marketplace early. That may be natural, but repeated actor sampling should not make this surface inevitable.

### Alternate-question answerability

Plausible paths include:

- migration constraints → sequencing → stopped automation → technical partnership;
- activation claim → denominator correction → decision consequence → leadership communication;
- implementation bottleneck → partner pilot → sample and opportunity cost → rotation;
- role-baseline portfolio scenario → actual migration evidence → independent leadership or technical dimension.

A good interview can defer the marketplace entirely and still reach a nuanced strategic and measurement profile. It can also test the marketplace without first prosecuting the resume number.

### Plausibility of short and corrected answers

Fatima's decision-first communication and confident headline are plausible. Her correction is admirably clean and may be too idealized: real candidates often need time, ask to verify, or give a provisional correction. Actor testing should include “I may have the denominator wrong; I need to check” where consistent with memory, without changing frozen final truth.

### Distinguishing good and bad interviewing

Good interviewing updates the active number, records both governance weakness and candid correction, tests one consequence, preserves macro coverage, and evaluates the marketplace conditionally. Bad interviewing keeps both numbers active, prosecutes integrity endlessly, treats the pilot as obviously brilliant, tests engineering internals, or requests protected commercial terms.

### More than one reasonable trajectory

After correction, measurement update, consequence, record-and-rotate, technical partnership, or leadership coverage may be reasonable. After marketplace emergence, outcome, opportunity cost, defer, or rotation are valid depending on prior coverage. The world explicitly supports a trajectory that never returns to the pilot.

### Preferred answer or question leakage

The exact denominator question in the example is powerful and may become an implicit gold question. Alternate phrasings—asking who was eligible, what happened to non-completers, or reconciling source tables—must also work. “Portfolio opportunity cost” and “macro coverage” are reviewer policy labels and should remain hidden.

### Nuanced hiring truth

The hidden profile supports strategic judgment, execution learning, and leadership communication; marks measurement integrity and technical partnership mixed; and preserves uncertainty around marketplace scale, metric-error pattern, and crisis leadership. The correction neither erases the error nor fixes the verdict.

### Can a weak interviewer game the world?

Yes. It can obtain the correction by asking the authored denominator question, reward polished strategy frameworks, or over-credit marketplace metrics. Conversely, it can force a negative result by calling the resume error fraud. Review must require source, consequence, and pattern uncertainty, not keywords or moral tone.

### Report fairness and interviewer failure

The report can distinguish the superseded claim, corrected truth, governance weakness, correction behavior, and untested pattern. If the interviewer spends the remaining session on integrity, missing technical and leadership evidence is interviewer-attributable. If the marketplace is ignored, scale remains uncertain rather than weak.

## Anti-overfitting audit

- **Tailoring to known Antigravity taxonomy:** Moderate risk. Correction lifecycle, emergent-surface value, opportunity cost, and coverage are explicit product concerns. The underlying B2B migration and partner constraints provide genuine human/work coherence beyond the control problem.
- **Repetitive shared skeleton:** Fatima's portfolio setting and numerical supersession are distinct. Yet the recurring requested bundle—strength, weakness, emergent surface, protected detail, irrelevant AI temptation—is clearly authored for the trial.
- **Labels leaking expected action:** “Corrected activation denominator,” “marketplace opportunity cost,” and family names tell reviewers how to interpret facts. They are hidden truth and must not enter actor or interviewer context.
- **Unnaturally dense edge cases:** High risk. Strong strategy, wrong resume number, candid correction, marketplace pilot, technical boundary, protected economics, and AI distraction coexist in a highly assessable package.
- **Lack of neutral facts:** Mitigated by career history, monthly review, implementation calls, organizational constraints, and mentoring. More ordinary roadmap work and an inconclusive customer problem would help.
- **World exists only to make one architecture fail:** No, but it is explicitly designed to test truth supersession and salience management. A human interviewer can still use several complete paths, and the world remains coherent without marketplace pursuit.

## Strongest aspects

- Metric governance and correction integrity remain distinct.
- Marketplace value is conditional on sample, selection, opportunity cost, and scale gates.
- Technical partnership is neither inflated nor dismissed.
- Macro coverage and residual uncertainty are explicit.

2. Marketplace expert review of pilot design, supply, quality, and economics.
3. Human interview review of whether the correction is implausibly clean.
4. Blind alternate-denominator questions not present in examples.
