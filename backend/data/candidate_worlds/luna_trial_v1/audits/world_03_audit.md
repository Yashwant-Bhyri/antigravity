# World 03 self-audit — Data Scientist

Status: strong exploratory candidate world; not gold.

## Coherence read

Amina's capabilities have a plausible lineage. Operations research led to predictive modeling; repeated forecasting work explains temporal validation and slice analysis; product experimentation was learned in a messier cross-functional environment where she did not control assignment. Fluent conceptual language is consistent with expertise and with overstatement. Monitoring ownership naturally grows from her diagnostic strength rather than appearing only as a rescue fact.

The weekly error review and team boundaries provide ordinary context. Still, the world is dense with a leakage repair, flawed experiment, monitoring incident, polished side project, protected data, and multiple communication traps. A human data-science reviewer should check whether the combined workload is plausible for one role and period.

## Required quality audit

### Internal contradiction

Strong predictive-model development and weak causal validity are separate dimensions. The 14% forecast-error reduction has stronger validation support than the 9% merchant outcome. Amina can correctly implement uplift methods while using data that does not support a strong treatment claim. Monitoring design can be personally owned while serving plumbing is not.

Risk: the resume's “uplift modeling” language may imply causal expertise inconsistent with the weakness. The intended distinction is mathematical implementation versus design validity; domain reviewers must confirm that this is credible rather than contrived.

### Ownership leakage

Amina owns features, validation, model analysis, monitoring definitions, slices, thresholds, and analytical recommendations. ML engineering owns deployment, online features, alert transport, rollback, and serving reliability. Product operations owns parts of merchant eligibility and rollout.

Risk: “designed monitoring” is easy to widen into MLOps ownership, while “developed experimentation strategy” can widen into assignment authority. Actor tests must cover both flattering and accusatory paraphrases.

### Future-answer leakage

The monitoring surface requires team context and a relevant trigger. Its incident requires monitoring ownership. The causal correction requires the outcome and experiment limitations. Feature leakage requires model-development context. Protected merchant details never become eligible.

Risk: the broad prompt “what is missing from your resume?” directly unlocks the monitoring surface and may make emergence too reliable. Alternate actor samples should sometimes volunteer a neutral work fact instead, consistent with behavior.

### Alternate-question answerability

Plausible paths include:

- forecast model → baseline → leakage repair → outcome;
- merchant ranking → assignment/denominator → corrected causal claim → prospective redesign;
- model operations → monitoring slices → schema-drift response → serving boundary;
- role-baseline modeling hypothetical → actual evidence → collaboration or communication.

The world also supports a complete interview that records monitoring without pursuing it. Model substance can be established through baseline, temporal split, error slice, or leakage repair rather than one ideal question.

### Plausibility of short and fluent answers

The fluent general answer is realistic and intentionally under-evidenced. The two-word causal headline followed by caveats is plausible. The strongest concrete examples are polished; voice rendering should include natural retrieval, approximation, and self-correction rather than delivering prose-quality paragraphs every time.

### Distinguishing good and bad interviewing

Good interviewing asks for one falsifiable artifact, preserves model strength, tests experiment validity sharply, and decides whether monitoring adds independent value. Bad interviewing rewards vocabulary, conducts endless algorithm trivia, lets one flawed experiment erase all competence, widens monitoring into serving, or tunnels into the wardrobe demo.

The distinction is strong, although a reviewer who sees hidden labels will know too much. Blind interviewer tests must omit latent and move metadata.

### More than one reasonable trajectory

After a fluent opening, artifact anchoring, model falsification, or another role dimension are reasonable. After the causal correction, a redesign, consequence test, record-and-rotate, or monitoring pivot are defensible. After monitoring emerges, one incident, a serving-boundary record, or rotation can all be good depending on coverage.

### Preferred answer or question leakage

The label “fluent but evidence-thin” appears in the original brief but not the world title; however, the hidden capability and audit naturally expose it. Family names such as `model_falsification` and `evidence_anchor` suggest reviewer behavior. They must remain excluded from interviewer context.

The promotion-leakage example is unusually crisp and can become an implicit gold answer. A reviewer should test alternate concrete model evidence, not require that incident.

### Nuanced hiring truth

The hidden profile supports model development and monitoring, marks experiment validity weak, causal reasoning mixed, and evidence communication mixed. Serving depth, prospective experiment design, and stakeholder influence remain uncertain. No binary verdict is encoded.

### Can a weak interviewer game the world?

Yes. Keywords such as temporal validation, calibration, leakage, uplift, and covariate shift are easily harvested. A weak interviewer can also “win” by asking the exact denominator question authored in the example. Human scoring must require a coherent chain from design to consequence, and evaluation should include novel phrasings.

### Report fairness and interviewer failure

A fair report must not collapse predictive and causal skill. If no sharp evidence request is made, modeling may be untested rather than weak. If the interviewer spends the session on algorithms, missing product judgment is partly interviewer-attributable. Protected merchant details and serving internals remain unavailable or unowned.

## Anti-overfitting audit

- **Tailoring to known Antigravity taxonomy:** Moderate risk. The world includes strong/weak contrast, emergent monitoring, consequence, and rotation concepts familiar to the product. It is less directly tied to one ownership route than World 02, but still clearly designed as a diagnostic landscape.
- **Repetitive shared skeleton:** The monitoring emergence differs from the other worlds, and fluent behavior is distinct. Yet the recurring structure—strong core, requested weakness, protected boundary, emergent strength, flashy distraction—is visible.
- **Labels leaking expected action:** “Model falsification,” “causal prosecution,” and high discriminative scores are oracle-like if exposed. Keep reviewer-only. The factual labels are mostly neutral.
- **Unnaturally dense edge cases:** High risk. Strong modeling, weak experiment validity, fluent abstraction, leakage repair, monitoring incident, serving boundary, and side-project distraction create a concentrated assessment world.
- **Lack of neutral facts:** Mitigated by career context, weekly error review, ordinary cross-functional roles, and two kinds of model work. More neutral unsuccessful iterations would help.
- **World exists only to make one architecture fail:** No. Several human interview paths work, and the world remains useful without the monitoring surface. Still, it is engineered to expose vocabulary over-credit and technical rabbit holes, so prevalence claims would be inappropriate.
