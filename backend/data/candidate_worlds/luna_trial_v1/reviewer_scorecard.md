# CandidateWorldV1 reviewer scorecard

Use this scorecard to decide whether a world is worth further calibration. A passing score does not make a world gold. Gold acceptance would additionally require independent domain review, actor replay, interviewer diversity testing, report-attribution testing, and a fresh holdout process.

## Review sequence

1. Read `authoring_calibration.md` and the world's resume and biography.
2. Read the evidence units without reading the example answers. Sketch two plausible interviews yourself.
3. Read the behavior profile and answer realizations. Check whether your alternate paths remain answerable.
4. Read the world audit and `evaluator_hidden_truth` last.
5. Score independently before discussing with another reviewer.

Do not judge a world by whether it makes the current Antigravity implementation look good. Judge whether it represents a coherent candidate reality that can expose good and bad interviewing across more than one path.

## Rating scale

- **0 — absent or invalid:** the requirement is missing or contradicted.
- **1 — weak:** present in name, but toy-like, scripted, or unreliable.
- **2 — usable with major revision:** some coherent evidence, with important gaps.
- **3 — strong exploratory material:** coherent and useful, with bounded weaknesses.
- **4 — exceptional:** independently convincing, richly alternate, and difficult to game.

Maximum score: 80.

## Decision bands

- **68–80:** strong candidate for independent calibration, not gold.
- **56–67:** useful exploratory world; revise before actor or interviewer comparison.
- **40–55:** conceptually useful but too brittle for evaluation.
- **Below 40:** reject or rewrite from the human story upward.

No world may advance with a score below 3 in human coherence, frozen identity, ownership fidelity, alternate answerability, multiple trajectories, report fairness, or domain plausibility, regardless of total.

## Hard rejection conditions

Reject the world immediately if any of these is true:

- A factual answer requires an uncited or nonexistent fact.
- Ownership widens through paraphrase or alternate questions.
- A protected detail is necessary to demonstrate the claimed capability.
- The candidate sees evaluator truth, move value, or the desired trajectory.
- A correction leaves both old and new claims active as equal truth.
- One exact next question is required to unlock the world.
- The world makes a salient surface obviously correct and all alternatives obviously bad.
- The world can only produce a fair report when the interviewer follows a scripted sequence.
- Candidate behavior changes competence or facts rather than access to speech.
- A weak interviewer can create missing evidence and the report inevitably blames the candidate.
- The role facts are implausible to a human domain reviewer.
- The world exists primarily to reproduce a named Antigravity bug, module, route, or architecture preference.

## Alternate-trajectory exercise

For each world, write three interview sketches before viewing its junctions:

1. **Resume-first:** start from the most salient resume claim, then cover one independent dimension.
2. **Role-first:** begin with a role baseline or hypothetical, then connect to resume evidence.
3. **Emergence-light:** do not pursue the emergent surface beyond recording it; see whether a nuanced report remains possible.

For each sketch, record:

- facts that became eligible;
- facts actually disclosed;
- dimensions supported, mixed, weak, untested, or unavailable;
- where interviewer failure could be falsely converted into candidate failure;
- why the chosen path was reasonable given time and prior evidence.

If only one sketch works, the world is too prescriptive.

## Actor review checks

Sample at least five actor responses per world:

- one broad question;
- one sharp but non-leading question;
- one repeated question;
- one unfair or protected-detail question;
- one alternate question not represented in `answer_realizations`.

Reject the actor/world pairing if any response invents facts, leaks evaluator truth, widens ownership, reveals a future fact, or uses protected details. Also reject if every sample converges on the same polished answer despite a behavior profile that should vary.

## Report review checks

A reviewer should be able to produce a final evidence profile with four distinct buckets:

- demonstrated strengths and weaknesses;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- untested or protected dimensions;
- interviewer-attributable failure.

If the report collapses those buckets into a single score or verdict, failure may belong to the report design rather than the world. The world is only good enough for further calibration when those attributions are possible from cited facts.

## Recommended human reviewers

Each world needs at least one practitioner in the target role and one interviewer or hiring manager. The junior world additionally needs a fairness-focused reviewer familiar with interview anxiety; the data-science world needs experiment-design review; the senior-PM world needs marketplace and B2B portfolio review; the backend world needs distributed-systems review; and the product-analyst world needs product-measurement review.
