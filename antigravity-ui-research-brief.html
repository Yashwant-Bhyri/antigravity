0. Read This First

This brief is meant to make you think, not to make you comply

The current UI prototypes are explorations. Please do not treat them as final. Your task is to understand the product, apply research and design judgment, and decide what the interface should become.

Decide how to segment the page, what to show, what to hide, where transcript belongs, how turn-taking is communicated, how the AI presence should behave, and how candidate trust is built.

1. Product Context

What Antigravity is

Antigravity is an AI-observed technical assessment platform. It evaluates engineering candidates through live reasoning under pressure, not through static quiz questions.

The live interview works like this:

  1. Candidate provides resume and role context.
  2. Backend prepares a resume-grounded interview plan.
  3. AI asks a spoken question.
  4. Candidate answers by speaking.
  5. System transcribes and analyzes the answer.
  6. AI asks an immediate, relevant follow-up.
  7. The cycle repeats until a report is generated.
The core UI problem: how do we make a live AI interview feel clear, intimate, trustworthy, and serious when there is no human interviewer physically present?

7. Interaction Scenarios

The final UI must handle these

Normal fluent candidate

AI asks, candidate answers well, answer commits, follow-up appears connected to prior answer.

Terse candidate

Candidate gives a very short answer; backend rescues with grounded follow-up.

Candidate interrupts AI

AI speech stops, candidate regains the floor, UI makes barge-in feel natural.

ASR error

Technical jargon is misheard; candidate may need correction affordance.

Backend latency

Next question is delayed; UI must make wait feel safe and intentional.

Closing

Final turn ends, report generation starts, room transitions gracefully.

8. Design Tensions

Think hard about these tradeoffs

  • Transcript transparency vs surveillance feeling
  • Immersion vs seriousness
  • Challenge vs psychological safety
  • AI transparency vs candidate anxiety
  • Human-like warmth vs honest machine identity
  • Current turn focus vs interview memory
  • Premium motion vs distraction
  • Progress visibility vs gamification
  • Candidate control vs assessment integrity
  • Simple UI vs enough recovery affordances
